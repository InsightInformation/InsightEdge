# Business Setup Checklist (Pennsylvania)

A plain-English list of what to set up to run Your Backup Plan legally and cleanly. Costs are approximate. **This is general guidance, not legal or tax advice.** A one-time meeting with a local CPA or tax preparer (~$150–300) to confirm the setup is money well spent.

## 1. Business structure: pick one

| Option | Cost | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Sole proprietor** + fictitious name | $70 filing + ~$100–250 to publish the notice in two newspapers | Cheapest, fastest way to start. Taxes flow through her personal return |
| **Single-member LLC** named "Your Backup Plan LLC" | $125 filing + $7 annual report | Separates business and personal liability. Looks more established to businesses and schools. Same pass-through taxes |

**Recommendation:** start as an **LLC**. It costs about the same as a sole proprietorship with a fictitious name (because the fictitious name also has to be published in newspapers), protects her personal assets if something goes wrong at a client's site, and makes vendor setup with schools and companies smoother.

- [ ] Check name availability: Pennsylvania Business Search (file.dos.pa.gov)
- [ ] File a Certificate of Organization online with the PA Department of State (file.dos.pa.gov), $125
- [ ] File the PA annual report each year ($7)
- [ ] *(If staying a sole proprietor instead: register the fictitious name "Your Backup Plan", $70, and publish the notice as required.)*

## 2. Tax IDs
- [ ] **EIN** (free, 10 minutes) at irs.gov. **Only use irs.gov.** Sites that charge for this are unnecessary. Use the EIN on W-9s instead of her Social Security number
- [ ] **PA business tax registration** (free) on myPATH (mypath.pa.gov). Ask whether her services need a sales tax license. **Note:** Pennsylvania taxes "help supply services" (temporary staffing where the worker is on the supplier's payroll but supervised by the client). A single owner doing her own work, directing how she does it, is usually treated differently. **Confirm with the PA Department of Revenue or a CPA before the first invoice.**
- [ ] **Local taxes:** register with her municipality's earned income tax collector (in the South Hills and Washington County this is usually **Jordan Tax Service** or **Keystone Collections Group**). Ask whether her town has a business privilege / mercantile tax or requires a business license

## 3. Money
- [ ] Separate **business checking account** (many local banks and credit unions have free small-business accounts). Every business dollar in and out goes through it
- [ ] A **tax savings account**: move **25–30%** of every payment there immediately
- [ ] **Quarterly estimated taxes** (federal via IRS Direct Pay, PA via myPATH): due **April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15**
- [ ] Track expenses (mileage log, phone, supplies, insurance, marketing, business share of internet). The free **Stride** or **Everlance** apps log mileage automatically
- [ ] Every business client paying her **$2,000+** in a year (2026 threshold) will ask for a **W-9** and send her a 1099-NEC. Keep her own records regardless

**Tax quick reference (2026, approximate):**
- Self-employment tax: **15.3%** on ~92% of net profit (Social Security + Medicare, both halves)
- Federal income tax: depends on household bracket. A **20% qualified business income deduction** usually applies
- PA personal income tax: **3.07%**
- Local earned income tax: ~**1–2%** depending on municipality
- **Retirement:** a SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) lets her save for retirement *and* cut taxes. Worth asking the CPA about in year one

## 4. Insurance
- [ ] **General liability insurance** (~$300–600/yr for a one-person admin/office service). Many businesses and every school will ask for a certificate of insurance. Get quotes from Next, Hiscox, The Hartford, or a local independent agent
- [ ] Consider **professional liability (E&O)** if handling records or bookkeeping-type data
- [ ] Consider a **fidelity / janitorial-type bond** (~$100–200/yr) if doing personal-assistant work in clients' homes. It reassures families
- [ ] Tell her **auto insurer** she drives for business (errands, supply runs). Personal policies may not cover business use

## 5. Clearances (for schools, youth organizations, churches)
Pennsylvania requires these for anyone with direct contact with children. **Renew every 5 years.** If she has current ones from the high school, check the dates and keep copies:
- [ ] **PA State Police criminal history** (Act 34), online, about $22
- [ ] **PA Child Abuse History** (Act 151), online, about $13
- [ ] **FBI fingerprint** (Act 114), through IdentoGO, about $25
- [ ] Mandated reporter training (Act 126 for schools), free online
- [ ] Schools may also require the **Act 168** employment history form and a TB test. Ask each district's business office

## 6. Paperwork to have ready
- [ ] **W-9** filled out with the EIN (PDF on hand to email)
- [ ] **Certificate of insurance** (PDF)
- [ ] **Clearances** (PDF)
- [ ] **Service agreement** (`service-agreement.md`)
- [ ] **Invoice template** with the business name and contact info, invoice number, dates and hours worked, payment terms and accepted payment methods

## 7. Stay an independent business (not an employee)
To keep things clean for both her and her clients:
- Invoice as Your Backup Plan. Don't go on a client's payroll
- Serve multiple clients and market to the public (the website and Facebook page help show this)
- Set her own rates, use her own phone and laptop where possible, and control *how* the work gets done
- Use a simple written agreement for every client (even one page)

## 8. Online presence
- [ ] Buy a domain, about $12–20/yr. Suggestions to check: **yourbackupplanpgh.com**, **yourbackupplanpa.com**, **yourbackupplan.co** *(check availability at Cloudflare, Porkbun or Namecheap)*
- [ ] Publish the website (see the main README)
- [ ] Optional: a professional email like `ashley@yourbackupplanpgh.com` through Google Workspace (~$7/mo) or free email forwarding through the domain registrar
