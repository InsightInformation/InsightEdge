# Pricing Guide

These are suggested starting rates. Ashley sets the final numbers. The logic behind them is laid out so she can adjust confidently.

## The market (Pittsburgh area, 2026)

| Reference point | Typical range | What it tells us |
|---|---|---|
| Employee admin assistant / receptionist wage, Pittsburgh metro | ~$18–24/hr | What the business pays its own staff, **before** payroll tax, benefits, PTO and hiring costs (usually +25–40%) |
| Temp agency **bill rate** for admin / reception (what the business pays the agency) | ~$28–40/hr | Her direct competitor. The agency keeps a large share of this |
| Freelance virtual / personal assistants | ~$30–50/hr | Independent, flexible help commands a premium |

**Positioning:** priced **at or slightly below a temp agency**, with no agency contract, no minimum week and a person they know by name. Businesses win on cost, and Ashley earns far more than an agency temp would pay her.

## Recommended rates

| Service | Rate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Business / office / school help** | **$35/hr** | 3-hour minimum per visit ($105) |
| **Full day** (up to 8 hrs on site) | **$260/day** | ~7% savings vs. hourly. Encourages bigger bookings |
| **Week block** (5 full days) | **$1,200/week** | For leave coverage and vacancies |
| **Personal assistant / household** (individuals) | **$32/hr** | 2-hour minimum. Organizing, errands, appointments, paperwork |
| **Same-day call-off coverage** | **+$10/hr** | Booked with less than 24 hours' notice. You're paying for the rescue |
| **Special projects** | **Flat quote** | Estimate the hours × $35, add 15% buffer. Clients love a fixed price |
| **Errand mileage** | IRS rate (**76¢/mile** for July–Dec 2026) | Only for driving *during* a job (errands, supply runs), not the commute to the client |
| **Nonprofits, churches, schools** | 10% off | Optional goodwill discount. These are great referral sources |

**"Backup on Call" plan (month 3+):** for a favorite client, **$150/month** retainer guarantees first priority for call-offs and includes 4 hours; extra hours at $35. It smooths out income and makes her the automatic first call.

## What she actually keeps

Self-employed people pay both halves of Social Security and Medicare, so a good rule of thumb is to **set aside 25–30% of every payment for taxes** (details in `launch-checklist.md`).

| Billable hours / week | Weekly revenue @ $35 | Yearly (48 weeks) | Set aside ~28% | Take-home (approx.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | $525 | $25,200 | $7,056 | ~$18,100 |
| 25 | $875 | $42,000 | $11,760 | ~$30,200 |
| 32 | $1,120 | $53,760 | $15,050 | ~$38,700 |

*Before business expenses (insurance, phone, marketing, mileage), which are mostly tax-deductible. Actual tax depends on her whole household situation, so confirm with a tax preparer.*

**Why not charge $22/hr like an employee wage?** Because as a business she covers her own taxes, has no paid time off, no benefits, no guaranteed hours and unbilled time spent marketing and driving. $35 as a business ≈ $22–25 as an employee. Undercharging is the #1 reason new service businesses fail.

## Raising prices

- Review rates every January. A $2–3/hr increase per year is normal.
- When she's regularly turning work away, raise rates for **new** clients first.
- Give existing clients 30 days' notice of any change.

## Getting paid

- **Invoice** weekly, or at job end for short jobs. Payment due within 15 days (businesses) or on completion (individuals).
- **Accept:** check, Venmo Business, Zelle, or card through Square / PayPal invoices (card fees are ~3%, so build that into pricing or add a small card fee).
- **Free invoicing:** Wave, Square Invoices, or the free tier of QuickBooks Solopreneur alternatives. A Google Sheets template works fine at first.
- Keep a simple log of **date · client · hours · amount · paid date** for every job. It makes taxes easy.
