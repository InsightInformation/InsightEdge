# Outreach Scripts

Social posts bring in friends of friends. **Direct outreach brings in the steady clients.** The best early targets are businesses where one person being out stops things from running:

- **Small offices:** dentists, chiropractors, vets, insurance agents, real estate offices, law and accounting firms (tax season!), property managers
- **Schools:** main offices, athletic departments, PTOs, private and parochial schools, preschools
- **Churches and nonprofits:** events, mailings, office coverage
- **Local retail and boutiques:** holiday rush, inventory counts
- **Small contractors and trades:** the owner's spouse usually runs the office alone
- **Busy families and seniors:** organizing, errands, appointments, paperwork

Start with people she already knows: former colleagues, parents from school, her own dentist and insurance agent.

## 1. Introduction email to a business

**Subject:** A local backup for when someone's out

> Hi [Name],
>
> My name is Ashley Harris. I live in [town] and recently started **Your Backup Plan**, a flexible helping-hand service for local businesses in the South Hills and Washington County.
>
> When someone calls off, an employee is out for a few days or weeks, or a project keeps getting pushed aside, I can step in for a few hours, a day, a week or a project. That includes front desk and phones, scheduling, data entry, filing, organizing, inventory and event help. There's no agency markup, no hiring paperwork and no long-term commitment.
>
> I'd love to be the person you call the next time you're short-handed. I've attached my flyer. Could I stop by for five minutes to introduce myself, or would it be easier to just keep my number on file?
>
> Thank you!
> Ashley Harris
> Your Backup Plan
> 412-401-3208 · yourbackupplan.ashley@gmail.com

>

> Ashley Harris · 412-401-3208

*Note: many districts can only pay outside help through a vendor or substitute process. Ask the business office: "What do you need from me to be set up as a vendor?" (usually a W-9, proof of insurance and clearances).*

## 3. Walk-in introduction (30 seconds)

Bring flyers and business cards. Go mid-morning Tuesday to Thursday, not Monday morning or lunch.

> "Hi! I'm Ashley, I live right in [town]. I just started a local business called Your Backup Plan. Basically, when a business is short-handed (someone calls off, someone's on leave, or there's a project nobody has time for), I come in and cover it, for a few hours or a few weeks. No agency, no hiring. Could I leave a flyer for whoever handles the office? And is there a best person or email to follow up with?"

Write down the manager's name. Follow up by email within 2 days.

## 6. After the job: thank you + review request

> Hi [Name]! Thank you so much for having me in this week. I really enjoyed helping out. If you were happy with my work, would you mind leaving a quick Google review? It helps other local businesses find me: [Google review link]
>
> And please keep my number handy for next time! ❤️ Ashley

*Get the review link from Google Business Profile → "Ask for reviews."*

---

## 7. Referral thank-you

> Thank you so much for referring [Name] to me! Referrals are the biggest compliment and the lifeblood of a small business. ❤️

Consider a small thank-you, such as a coffee gift card or one free hour of help, for referrals that turn into booked work.

---

## 8. Answering "What do you charge?"

> "It depends on the kind of work. For most business and office help, it's $[rate] an hour with a [3]-hour minimum, and I'm happy to give you a flat quote for a project. Tell me a little about what you need and I'll give you an exact number."
